I wrote this as a general foundation and to store my research into nutrition and weight gain after spending my entire life being skinny and never eating seriously enough to gain weight. I'm storing this here as an easy reference and so hopefully someone can learn and have a reference for their personal goals.

# Scope
To put things in perspective, we'll estimate my BMR (basal metabolic rate) using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, since I don't really know my *bf*%

$BMR = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age (years)} + 5$
$BMR = 10 \times 77.1 + 6.25 \times 198 - 5 \times 23 + 5$
**$BMR = 771 + 1237.5 - 115 + 5 = 1898.5\text{ calories per day}$**

I know some people that don't eat that many calories in a day, and thats the energy required to keep me alive. Couple that with my active lifestyle going to the gym every day, Total energy expenditure looks more like 3200 cal/day.

*A safe estimate for calorie intake would be in the 4000-4500 range*

Anyone who has tracked their calorie intake in a day knows that amount of food isn't exactly going to be easy if I want to hit my macros too.

# Inspiration
I'll be leaning heavily on the teachings of Sam Sulek and Rich Piana for this protocol, they like to eat pretty much nonstop throughout the day and I think that will be the most manageable method for success. Piana says start with 4 meals a day and add a meal per week until you hit 15, I will be starting with 4 and probably capping at 8 meals per day. Update will come in February. I'm going to be focusing on eating real foods, I will obviously be supplementing with protein powder, and the occasional fast food "tactical cheat" to add a quick 1200-2000 calories. My brother would famously get the $40 panda express family meal (probably 4-6k calories) and crush that in one sitting. I don't have a panda express near me but I will be attempting similar feats and testing their long term effects.

# Macros
Protein targets should be very achievable due to sheer volume of food, and my natural inclination towards meats and protein dense foods. 
Target is 200g/day.
Carbs stimulate insulin release and replenish glycogen in the muscle, pre and post workout carbs are essential for fueling and making sure I'm not depleted after. Being a larger human, I can store more carbs than someone smaller.
Target is 500-600g/day.
Fats have 9 calories per gram, making them the easiest way to up your calorie intake. All the good fats like in eggs and avocados and fish are the precursors to testosterone which is also really ideal for growth
Target is 100g/day

# Logistics
Like I mentioned earlier, I'll be splitting my calories into multiple meals over the course of the day, which means I'll be eating 4-8 meals, making cooking each one of them a logistical impossibility if I want to have any hope of being productive. This being said, I'll be focusing on "instant" or "minute" foods that require next to no effort and meal prepping. Costco and other bulk warehouses are great for this because you can get giant boxes of cereal, oats, eggs, microwave rice cups, greek yogurt, all sorts of things that make the prepping a lot easier (don't forget the rotisserie chicken). # Digestive efficiency
Now we get into the adjacent things that you need to plan for when eating an inhuman amount of food, constantly pushing yourself past the point of enjoyment in your meals. If you don't take care of your gut and try to eat that much, all you're going to do is bloat yourself and ruin your progress. Fermented foods are great for this, I like Kimchi but whatever works for you is fine. Be careful of "probiotics" because 90% of the time they're just snake oil, real probiotics need to be refrigerated to preserve the culture and I don't know of any pills being sold in the refrigerator section.
Exogenous Digestive enzymes are a really great idea, they'll help break down those giant meals a lot faster, I'll be buying some of those, Pineapple also has naturally occuring enzymes so adding that to a shake helps out.
Fiber is gonna be really important but I'm gonna be fading a lot of vegetables just because I don't want them occupying valuable real estate in my stomach. This is not to exception, I'm just not going to think about them at all. If its in my food then fine but I'm not going out of my way to get them in.
# Water
Most people don't come close to drinking enough water, and I really only see bodybuilders taking water consumption seriously because it makes such a difference on your size and strength. Muscle fibers (and the body) are 75% water. I'm not sure what they tell you in middle school about water consumption but I made it easy for myself, I have a liter water bottle and I refill it 6 times a day. This will be increasing to 8 as I get closer to 200lbs. Electrolytes are essential for transporting the water into the muscle cells so supplement with a scoop if you got money, otherwise a salt grinder and some potassium works great. Farther along I'll be supplementing my workouts with some sort of carbohydrate drink but I don't feel that to be a critical step for growth right now.

# Final Thoughts
The TLDR here is just eat more, but the mental barriers 
This level of weight gain is really going to suck, so it really has to be treated as part of the training itself, just like the gym. Eating isn't really going to be enjoyable, just a means to an end. When weight gain stalls, add another meal, or add some olive oil. I'm approaching this as progressive overload, just like my lifts, seeing how far I can push my stomach and then pushing it farther. Mentality here is **Whatever it takes**, and its going to take a whole lot
